## 1. Brand in one page

**AiR is a free, mindset-first AI learning community for ages eight through adult.** It turns complex AI work into simple human moves: choose a purpose, give clear direction, challenge the result, and release something useful.[1] [2]

> **Stop learning AI. Learn to move with it.**

| Brand decision | AiR standard |
|---|---|
| **Brand idea** | Human judgment in motion |
| **Purpose** | Help people use AI with purpose, clarity, judgment, and action |
| **Promise** | Turn a complex AI task into one clear next move |
| **Category** | Free AI-readiness learning and practice community |
| **Audience** | Learners ages eight through adult, families, educators, creators, community organizations, and working teams |
| **Difference** | AiR teaches a durable human method instead of a temporary list of tools or “magic prompts” |
| **Enemy** | Hype, tool chasing, passive watching, false certainty, and endless preparation |
| **Behavior** | Make one move. Check it. Share what changed. |
| **Master line** | Stop learning AI. Learn to move with it. |
| **Short campaign line** | Move with it. |
| **Institutional line** | Free community learning · Digi2U.org |

The internal ambition is to become the most recognizable action brand in AI learning: culturally relevant, emotionally direct, visually ownable, and built around practice. That ambition should shape the work, but comparisons to other commercial brands should **not** appear in public-facing AiR copy.

## 2. The brand platform

### 2.1 The human truth

People do not need more pressure to “keep up with AI.” They need a way to decide what matters, what to ask, what to trust, and what to make. Children and adults can learn the same judgment loop when the entry point is concrete and the action is clear.[2]

### 2.2 The cultural tension

AI communication often makes people feel late, replaceable, or dependent on secret tricks. AiR rejects that frame. The machine may be fast, but **the human still chooses the mission, defines quality, checks consequences, and owns the result**.

### 2.3 The transformation

| Before AiR | After AiR |
|---|---|
| “I need to learn every tool.” | “I need one useful mission.” |
| “The right prompt will solve it.” | “Clear direction creates better options.” |
| “It sounds confident, so it must be right.” | “Confidence is not evidence.” |
| “I am still getting ready.” | “I made a version and learned from a person.” |
| “AI is the expert.” | “AI drafts. Humans decide.” |

### 2.4 The four-move method

The four moves are the spine of the product, the curriculum, the community, and the campaign system.[2]

| Move | Human question | Public promise | Campaign verb |
|---|---|---|---|
| **01 · Clear** | Who am I helping, and what should change? | Purpose before tools | Choose |
| **02 · Direct** | What context, task, limits, and finish line does the machine need? | Clarity before cleverness | Direct |
| **03 · Judge** | What might be wrong, unfair, unsafe, private, or unfit? | Check before you trust | Challenge |
| **04 · Make** | What can I finish, test, improve, and release? | Finish the loop | Release |

### 2.5 Brand character

| AiR is | AiR is not |
|---|---|
| Bold | Loud for attention’s sake |
| Direct | Cold or dismissive |
| Athletic | Aggressive or macho |
| Warm | Cute, childish, or patronizing |
| Rigorous | Academic, bureaucratic, or jargon-heavy |
| Optimistic | Naive about risk |
| Simple | Shallow |
| Community-led | Popularity-driven |

## 3. Audience system

AiR uses **one mindset with different doors**, not separate “kid” and “adult” brands. Explore, Create, and Build change the support and depth without ranking people by age.[2]

| Audience | Tension | What AiR gives them | Show them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Young learner, 8+** | “AI feels like magic or a test I could fail.” | Familiar stories, one action at a time, room to ask why | Drawing, speaking, sorting, making, testing with a trusted adult |
| **Parent or caregiver** | “I want a safe, useful way to learn together.” | Privacy guidance, shared activities, no grades, no pressure | Side-by-side learning, consent, conversation, real-world usefulness |
| **Educator or trainer** | “I need a method that works across ability levels.” | Repeatable lesson beats, presentation mode, facilitation guides | Rooms in motion, discussion, paper activities, live reflection |
| **Creator or professional** | “I need practical results without surrendering judgment.” | Briefing, options, verification, workflows, release | Work in progress, critique, revision, delivery to an audience |
| **Community organization** | “Our people need access, not hype.” | Free entry, mixed-age learning, community practice | Local rooms, trusted leaders, shared screens, useful community outcomes |

## 5. Voice and writing

### 5.1 Voice rules

AiR sounds like a trusted coach standing beside the learner: calm, specific, honest, and ready to move.

| Rule | Do | Do not |
|---|---|---|
| **Verbs first** | Choose one mission. | The selection of a mission should occur. |
| **One idea at a time** | Check the date. Then check the source. | Conduct a multidimensional verification process. |
| **Short sentences** | AI makes guesses. You decide what to use. | Artificial intelligence probabilistically generates outputs that require subsequent human validation. |
| **Second person** | You choose what good means. | Users should establish quality criteria. |
| **Concrete language** | Circle the claim you need to check. | Engage in critical evaluation. |
| **Respect the learner** | Start simple. Go deeper when you are ready. | This is the easy version for kids. |
| **Name limits** | AI can sound sure and still be wrong. | AI will transform everything. |

### 5.2 Reading test

Every public line should pass three questions:

1. Can an eight-year-old explain the main idea after hearing it once?
2. Would an adult say the sentence aloud without feeling talked down to?
3. Does the copy point to a choice, check, or action?

### 5.3 Word bank

| Use often | Use carefully | Do not use |
|---|---|---|
| move, mission, choose, direct, check, make, try, show, change, person, useful, real, together | prompt, automation, workflow, system, model, risk | leverage, utilize, mastery, competency framework, learning objectives, assessment criteria, best-in-class, revolutionary, magic prompt, replace people |

### 5.4 Naming standard

Use **AiR — AI Readiness** on first mention in formal materials. Use **AiR** afterward. The official mark contains the lowercase **i**, and its inner shapes are negative space. Use **Digi2U.org** in institutional footers and partner credits. Do not redraw the name with an unrelated italic typeface.

### 6.1 Official files

| Asset | Use |

| [`AiR_Logo-MAIN.png`](https://borngifted.github.io/air/media/AiR_Logo-MAIN.png) | Raster fallback for software that cannot place SVG |

### 6.2 What the mark means

The forward italic communicates motion before the eye finishes reading the letters. Its weight creates confidence. Its open counters create breathing room. The lowercase **i** lives in the negative space between **A** and **R**, turning the idea of human presence into the center of the mark.

### 6.3 Color lockups

| Background | Visible mark | Negative space |
|---|---|---|
| Deep Air or Ink | Paper `#F7FFF8` | Must match the exact background color |
| Paper or Warm Ground | Deep Air `#132A24` | Must match the exact background color |
| Photography or video | Place the correct mark on a clean solid field, or use a frame where the background behind the entire mark is visually uniform | Must not fill with a third color |

The center of the lowercase **i** and the inner shapes of **A** and **R** must always read as cutouts into the background. Never fill those shapes with a decorative color.

### 6.4 Clear space and minimum size

Use the circular dot of the **i** as the measurement unit **x**. Keep at least **1x** clear space on every side of the mark. For small or visually noisy placements, increase clear space to **2x**.

| Placement | Minimum |
|---|---:|
| Digital mark | 40 px wide |
| Digital mark with “AiR · AI Readiness” text | 120 px wide |
| Print mark | 12 mm wide |
| Print lockup | 30 mm wide |

### 6.5 Never do this

Do not stretch, outline, bevel, glow, shadow, rotate arbitrarily, add a gradient, recolor the **i**, add a permanent background plate, place the mark over a face, or use the mark as a decorative pattern so often that it loses meaning. A subtle shadow is acceptable only when it separates the approved single-color lockup from moving film footage; it must not alter the geometry.

## 7. Color system

### 7.1 Core palette

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Deep Air** | `#132A24` | Canonical brand ground, primary mark, hero surfaces |
| **Paper** | `#F7FFF8` | Reading surfaces, type on Deep Air, logo cutout ground |
| **Go** | `#18C98B` | Buttons, active states, progress, “move now” signal |
| **Spark** | `#D8FF45` | One high-energy highlight, focus ring, decisive reveal |
| **Ink** | `#0B1613` | Deepest contrast, video stages, grounding text |
| **Mist** | `#C9DED4` | Secondary copy and calm separators on dark ground |
| **Deep Soft** | `#355047` | Secondary copy on pale surfaces |
| **Warm Ground** | `#F2F3E9` | Editorial page background |
| **Raised Paper** | `#FFFDF6` | Cards and physical-feeling surfaces |
| **Coral Signal** | `#FF715B` | Warnings and meaningful risk only; never a decorative fifth accent |

### 7.2 Color behavior

Deep Air and Paper do most of the work. Go is a verb. Spark is a shout. Coral is a warning.

| Color | Recommended share |
|---|---:|
| Deep Air / Ink | 45–55% |
| Paper / Warm Ground / Raised Paper | 30–40% |
| Go | 8–12% |
| Spark | 3–5% |
| Coral Signal | Under 2% |

Never set Go text on Paper for body copy. Never place Go and Spark text on one another. On dark surfaces, use Paper or Mist for copy; reserve Go and Spark for actions, icons, rules, focus, and one key phrase.

## 8. Typography

### 8.1 Families

| Role | Typeface | Weights | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Display** | Barlow Condensed | 600, 700, 800; 800 italic | Headlines, numbers, path names, end cards |
| **Body** | Manrope | 400, 500, 600, 700, 800 | Paragraphs, UI, captions, scripts, instructions |

Fallbacks are `Impact, sans-serif` for display and `system-ui, sans-serif` for body.

### 8.2 Typesetting

Display headlines are condensed, uppercase, tightly spaced, and usually two to six words. Use italic display for the human turn: **LEARN TO MOVE WITH IT.** Body copy should begin at 17 px for reading experiences, use generous line height, and stay under roughly 66 characters per line. Do not set long paragraphs in all caps.

### 8.3 Commercial typography

On-screen copy should be readable without sound. Use no more than six words per frame, keep text away from faces and platform UI zones, and hold every major line long enough to read aloud once. Use Paper on Deep Air by default; use Spark for only one word or one end-card signal.

## 9. Composition and graphic language

AiR combines **athletic editorial design with community warmth**.[2] The visual system should feel trained, not corporate; energetic, not frantic.

| Device | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Scale** | Oversized headlines and 01–04 numerals create confidence and navigation |
| **Direction** | Forward slant, cropped mark, diagonal movement, or a subject moving through frame |
| **Structure** | Strong grid, generous negative space, clear reading order |
| **Shapes** | Pill actions, circular AiR dot, soft rectangular drills, occasional orbit line |
| **Texture** | Paper, marker, fabric, skin, screens in use, real rooms—not synthetic sci-fi effects |
| **Depth** | One foreground action, one human subject, one readable background layer |

Never make AiR look like a generic learning-management system, a futuristic robot company, or a neon “cyber” interface. Avoid graduation caps, light bulbs, floating brains, chrome robots, hologram hands, generic blue technology grids, and stock-photo handshakes.

## 10. Photography and casting

### 10.1 Human standard

Black and brown people should be central, specific, and active—not background diversity. Show intergenerational learning, with children ages eight and older, caregivers, educators, creators, and working adults. The campaign world should feel like a real community room, studio, kitchen table, classroom, library, barbershop back room, nonprofit workspace, or creative office.

### 10.2 What people are doing

Prioritize hands drawing, speaking, pointing, crossing out, comparing, checking a source, moving cards, filming, revising, testing, and showing work to another person. A screen may be present, but the story must not be “person staring at laptop.”

### 10.3 Image direction

| Dimension | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Light** | Directional daylight or motivated practical light; warm skin, deep green shadows |
| **Camera** | Human-height, close enough to feel participation; 35 mm and 50 mm perspectives |
| **Movement** | Controlled handheld, slow push, purposeful lateral movement, confident lock-off for decisions |
| **Wardrobe** | Deep greens, cream, black, denim, brown, with one Go or Spark accent |
| **Props** | Paper, marker, tape, sticky notes, printed sources, phone, laptop, camera, simple prototypes |
| **Expression** | Concentration, recognition, laughter from discovery, calm confidence—not staged amazement |

For child talent, obtain guardian consent, use age-appropriate work hours, protect private information, and avoid identifiable school, address, location, or account details. Do not show a child alone using an unmoderated public AI service.

## 11. Motion and sound

### 11.1 Motion character

AiR moves forward. Transitions should feel like decisions: quick, grounded, and reversible. Interface motion lives mostly between 160 and 250 milliseconds. Film motion should alternate between active human making and decisive stillness when the learner chooses or checks.

The four-beat brand rhythm is:

> **Choose. Direct. Check. Make.**

Use four edits, four taps, four marker strokes, or four percussive hits to create a repeatable mnemonic. Do not use aggressive glitching, fake code rain, or excessive speed ramps.

### 11.2 Sound character

Commercial sound may use room tone, breath, pencil, marker, paper, keyboard, camera shutter, table taps, and a restrained four-beat percussion bed. Music should feel modern and kinetic without sounding like a technology demo. Avoid robotic voices, futuristic bleeps, inspirational corporate ukulele, or “epic” trailer swells.

AiR does not use a podcast, radio, or audio-only product experience. All commercial dialogue must be captioned, and every visual idea must still make sense with sound off.

### 11.3 Mark reveal

The preferred motion mark starts with two dark or pale forward strokes. The circular **i** dot arrives on the fourth beat. Hold the complete mark for at least two seconds on the end card. Do not assemble the mark with sparks, smoke, chrome, or 3D extrusion.

## 12. Campaign system

The campaign is not four disconnected ads. It is one human method seen through four decisions.

| Film | Tension | Human move | Closing line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **01 · Clear** | Noise creates paralysis | Choose one person and one change | Purpose before tools. |
| **02 · Direct** | Vague requests create random guesses | Define audience, task, limits, and finish line | You choose what good means. |
| **03 · Judge** | Fluent output creates false trust | Check claims, stakes, fit, and consequences | Check before you trust. |
| **04 · Make** | Endless preparation hides the work | Finish a small version and show a real person | Put it in the world. |

### 12.1 Thirty-second story spine

| Time | Function |
|---:|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Human tension visible before explanation |
| 0:03–0:07 | Name the bad pattern: noise, vagueness, false confidence, or delay |
| 0:07–0:18 | Show the AiR move in hands, language, and behavior |
| 0:18–0:25 | Show a useful change in the work or another person |
| 0:25–0:30 | End card: move number, line, logo, free CTA |

### 12.2 Required end card

Every campaign film ends on Deep Air or Paper with the correct negative-space mark.

**Primary end card copy:**

> **STOP LEARNING AI.**  
> ***LEARN TO MOVE WITH IT.***

**Footer:** `FREE FOR EVERYONE · AGES 8 TO ADULT`  
**CTA:** `JOIN AIR FREE · borngifted.github.io/air/`

## 14. Accessibility and inclusion standard

AiR’s design must support the same idea across reading level, age, device, and ability. Captions are mandatory. Important meaning cannot depend on color alone. Avoid fast flashes, tiny text, busy backgrounds behind captions, or voiceover that introduces essential facts not shown visually. Use visible focus, high contrast, plain language, and reduced-motion alternatives.[2] [4] [5]

The work should show access rather than merely claim it: large type, shared devices, paper alternatives, captions, keyboard use, different ages, and people checking one another’s understanding.

## 15. Approval checklist

Before any AiR asset goes live, the reviewer should be able to answer **yes** to every question.

| Check | Approval question |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Is one human outcome clear? |
| **Age range** | Can an eight-year-old understand the central idea without making the adult audience feel patronized? |
| **Action** | Does the asset show a choice, check, or act of making? |
| **Judgment** | Does the machine remain a draft-maker rather than an authority? |
| **Representation** | Are Black and brown people central, active, and dimensional? |
| **Logo** | Are the lowercase i and inner A/R shapes true negative space matching the background? |
| **Color** | Is Spark used once, with no green-on-green copy? |
| **Type** | Is every line readable at the intended size and duration? |
| **Access** | Are captions, contrast, safe-area, and reduced-motion needs covered? |
| **CTA** | Does the asset say AiR is free and point to one clear next move? |
| **Integrity** | Does the asset avoid fake reviews, impossible claims, fear, or guarantees? |

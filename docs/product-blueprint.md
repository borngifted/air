# AiR Product Blueprint

## Product promise

**AiR helps anyone—from age eight onward—turn a complex AI task into one clear next move.** The platform does not teach people to chase tools or memorize prompts. It teaches a durable mindset: choose a human goal, give useful direction, inspect what comes back, improve it with judgment, and put the result into the world.

> **Clear the noise. Make something useful. Learn what only you can decide.**

AiR is free. It welcomes children, families, educators, creators, community organizations, and working adults into the same learning system without making younger learners feel behind or experienced learners feel talked down to.

## The simple-to-deep learning model

Every lesson follows the same five-part rhythm. This consistency lowers cognitive load while allowing the subject matter to become more sophisticated.

| Beat | Child-facing language | Learning function |
|---|---|---|
| See it | “Here is a story you already understand.” | Begin with a concrete situation, visual, or everyday analogy |
| Name it | “Here is the one big idea.” | Introduce one plain-language concept and only the vocabulary needed now |
| Try it | “Make one small move.” | Complete a guided choice, short reflection, or practical exercise |
| Check it | “Look again. What could be wrong?” | Practice verification, fairness, privacy, audience fit, and human judgment |
| Share it | “Tell the community what changed.” | Turn private learning into reflection and constructive discussion |

The lesson interface will never present a child with a wall of instructions. Each checkpoint asks for one action, gives one worked example, and explains why the action matters. A learner can always choose **Explore**, **Create**, or **Build**.

| Entry | Experience | Intended learner need |
|---|---|---|
| Explore | Guided choices, read-aloud copy, familiar examples, and short creative play | First-time learners, children, families, and anyone who wants more support |
| Create | Real project decisions, audience definition, drafting, review, and sharing | Most independent learners |
| Build | Systems thinking, workflow design, automation, production, and deeper technical context | Advanced learners without age gating |

## Learning architecture

The complete curriculum is organized into four paths and twelve lessons. Each path ends with a useful artifact, while every lesson includes a video, story, interactive prompt, reflection checkpoint, action exercise, and community discussion invitation.

| Path | Promise | Lessons | Learner evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **01 · Clear** | Replace AI noise with one human purpose | Clear the Air; Machines Make Guesses; Choose One Mission | A one-sentence mission and a simple explanation of what AI can and cannot know |
| **02 · Direct** | Turn an idea into clear directions | Be the Director; Give Useful Context; Ask for Options | A reusable brief with audience, task, boundaries, and a check |
| **03 · Judge** | Know when an output is useful, wrong, unfair, or unsafe | Spot the Guess; Check the Stakes; Make It Fit People | A documented review showing facts checked, risks noticed, and human changes made |
| **04 · Make** | Build, improve, and release work that serves someone | Make a First Version; Build Your Way; Put It in the World | A finished artifact, a repeatable process, and a reflection shared with the community |

Progress is shown as **moves completed** and **work made**. The learner dashboard highlights the current path, next lesson, last reflection, finished exercises, and community activity. It avoids grades, leaderboards, streak pressure, and public comparison.

## Interactive video lesson model

Videos are short chapters rather than passive lectures. The player pauses at authored checkpoints and opens a focused activity alongside or below the video.

| Moment | Interaction | Persistence |
|---|---|---|
| Before watching | One prediction or familiar-story question | Optional response saved to the lesson attempt |
| During the idea | A single choice, sort, or “what would you do?” prompt | Checkpoint response and completion state |
| During the example | An annotation or comparison between two approaches | Selected reasoning saved for resume |
| After the example | A short reflection in the learner’s own words | Private reflection by default |
| Practice | Explore, Create, or Build exercise | Exercise response and completion state |
| Next action | Complete lesson, continue, or join the linked discussion | Progress update and discussion context |

Videos require visible captions and a transcript. The experience will include video controls only; it will not include any podcast, audio-only program, radio section, or standalone audio player.

## Community model

The AiR community is a practice space, not a popularity feed. Discussions are organized around four prompts: **What did you try? What surprised you? What did you change? What will you do next?** Members can publish posts and comments, view a learner’s public contributions, and enter discussions directly from a lesson.

To support younger members safely, public profiles use a chosen display name and optional broad role such as Learner, Parent, Educator, or Creator. Public profiles do not display age, email, location, school, or private reflections. The first release will not include direct messages, private groups, follower counts, or location features. Posts use structured categories and include an accessible report action; administrators can review and remove content.

## Onboarding and profile model

After account creation, onboarding asks only what improves the learning experience: a public display name, broad role, preferred learning mode, first path, and one thing the learner hopes to make. A child-facing privacy note explains that they should not share their full name, school, address, phone number, or other private details in community posts.

The profile stores learning-path enrollment, lesson progress, checkpoint responses, private reflections, finished artifacts as text or links, posts, comments, and onboarding choices. Returning members land on **Continue your move**, which resumes the most recent incomplete lesson.

## Visual system

AiR retains its canonical mark and colors while developing a richer system around them.

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Deep Air | `#132A24` | Primary ground, navigation, hero surfaces |
| Paper | `#F7FFF8` | Reading surfaces and high-contrast type |
| Go | `#18C98B` | Actions, active states, completed moves |
| Spark | `#D8FF45` | One high-energy highlight per screen |
| Ink | `#0B1613` | Deep contrast and video surfaces |
| Mist | `#C9DED4` | Secondary copy and calm borders |

The aesthetic combines athletic editorial design with community warmth. Headlines are oversized, condensed, and forward-moving; body copy is plain, generous, and highly legible. Slanted cuts and the AiR dot create motion. Cards behave like training drills rather than academic course tiles. Photography and video focus on hands, making, rehearsal, correction, and people sharing work.

## Trainer knowledge base model

The trainer knowledge base is a separate authenticated route family designed for preparation and delivery. It is not linked as a learner lesson. Each resource includes the lesson promise, the one big idea, age-eight explanation, adult extension, preparation checklist, room setup, timing, read-aloud script, demonstration, Explore/Create/Build variations, discussion prompts, likely misconceptions, accessibility and no-device adaptation, child-safety notes, source references, and post-session reflection.

| Resource type | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Facilitator guide | Run a complete lesson or workshop |
| Teaching framework | Understand the reasoning behind the AiR method |
| Exercise card | Print or reuse one activity independently |
| Delivery note | Adapt for age, group size, time, devices, or accessibility |
| Source reference | Verify research, examples, and responsible-use claims |
| Video guide | Prepare lesson video, pause points, captions, and discussion cues |

## Acceptance principles

An eight-year-old should be able to explain the lesson’s main idea after hearing the opening story. An adult should still encounter a meaningful decision or tradeoff. Every lesson must produce a visible action, not merely content consumption. Every AI output must be framed as a draft or prediction rather than an authority. Every path must connect private practice to constructive community reflection. Every public experience must state that AiR is free. No podcast or audio-player experience may appear anywhere in the platform.

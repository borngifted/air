# Environment Designer Training (optional warm-ups)

**Kept for the optional warm-ups only.** The AiR program is [AiR: Flow With AI](air-flow-with-ai.md): the one-hour class, the four moves, and the Prompt Relay. Read that first. Use the situations below only if you want a short warm-up before the class; they are not the method, and the class does explain the mission and the moves.

Original handbook follows.

You are not a teacher. You are an Environment Designer. Your job is to design conditions where discovery happens, then get out of the way and watch.

---

## Part 1 · The mindset you bring

**The one rule.** You do not explain AI. Not at the start, not when someone asks, not at the end. If you catch yourself explaining prompting, stop mid-sentence and turn it into a situation.

**Don’t ask questions. Create decisions.** “How do you feel about AI?” is a survey. Three spots on the floor and ten seconds is data.

**Confusion is a tool.** School removes confusion. You create safe confusion on purpose. Safe means nobody is humiliated and nobody is stuck alone. Confused means nobody is handed the answer.

**AI is furniture.** It is one object in the room, next to the markers and the camera. It is never the subject.

**Behavior beats answers.** You will leave the room with photos, notes, and discarded attempts, not with correct answers.

**The test.** Before you run anything, ask: could this activity exist in a normal classroom? If yes, redesign it.

---

## Part 2 · Before the room

### The space

Remove the rows. If you cannot move furniture, push it to the edges and use the floor.

Set up, in this order:

1. **Three stations** marked on the floor with tape or paper: DO IT MYSELF · DO IT WITH AI · LET AI DO IT. Far enough apart that choosing means walking.
2. **Three walls** with big words: I KNOW · I THINK I KNOW · I HAVE NO IDEA.
3. **Materials everywhere**, not in one place: markers, paper, tape, cardboard, objects, a camera or phones, headphones, at least one screen with AI open, at least one screen with no AI, QR codes that lead to odd places, one piece of unfinished artwork.
4. **Strange instructions** on cards around the room. Some true, some misleading, some impossible.
5. **Your capture kit**: a phone for photos, a notebook, a box or folder for discarded attempts, a way to collect voice notes.

### What you prepare

Only conditions. Never content.

| Prepare | Do not prepare |
|---|---|
| The challenge sentence (one line) | Slides |
| Which solution you will remove (the disrupt) | A “What is AI?” intro |
| The pairs you will collide | A vocabulary sheet |
| The mystery output for controlled confusion | Example answers |
| The statements for the walls | A rubric |
| The evidence you will watch for | A grade |

### The challenge

One sentence. It must have a real person on the other side.

> Make one thing today that helps one real person.

Variants: a page your grandmother can read; a thirty-second video for next year’s freshmen; a sign for the corner store; a schedule for someone who texts you forty times a day.

## Part 3 · Run of show (about 90 minutes)

| Time | Action | What you do | What you capture |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | **Expose** | No welcome speech. Point at the floor. “Three stations. You have ten seconds. Choose.” Count down out loud. | Photo of where everyone stands. Who hesitated. Who followed a friend. |
| 0:02 | **Expose** | Read the five statements one at a time. People walk to a wall. Say nothing about who is right. | Photo after each statement. |
| 0:08 | **Explore** | Read the challenge once. “Use anything in this room.” Then stop talking. | First action of each person. First AI request, word for word. |
| 0:25 | **Disrupt** | Watch what most people reached for. Remove it. AI gone, or search gone, or written prompts gone. Say it once, no reason given. | Who switches tools. Who freezes. What gets abandoned. |
| 0:40 | **Collide** | Re-pair people who would not choose each other. Or hand a human draft to an AI-first person and vice versa. “Both of you have to be able to say why it is good.” | Who leads. Which tool gets picked first. Questions people ask each other. |
| 0:55 | **Flip** | “For fifteen minutes, AI cannot answer you. It can only ask you questions.” | Whose idea got sharper. Who got frustrated. |
| 1:10 | **Reflect** | No worksheet. “Leave evidence.” Screenshots, voice notes, the thing you threw away, a photo of the mess. | What people save. What they choose to show. |
| 1:20 | **Evolve** | Repeat the exact opening. “Three stations. Ten seconds. Choose.” Then the same five statements at the walls. | Photo. Compare with 0:00. Who moved. |
| 1:28 | Close | One sentence: “The photos are the lesson. Same time next week.” Leave. | Count of finished things. |

Never cut Expose or Evolve. Without both, you have no data.

---

## Part 4 · Scripts you say out loud

Keep these short. Say them once. Do not explain them.

- **Opening:** “Three stations. Ten seconds. Choose.”
- **The walls:** “‘AI is creative.’ Go stand where you belong.”
- **The challenge:** “Make one thing today that helps one real person. Use anything in this room.”
- **The disrupt:** “AI is closed for the next fifteen minutes.” (No reason.)
- **The reference-searcher:** “If you are looking at somebody else’s solution, how are you going to make something you have never seen?”
- **The AI condition:** “You can use it. You cannot ask it for an example.”
- **The flip:** “For fifteen minutes AI cannot answer you. It can only ask.”
- **When someone asks what AI is:** “What did you reach for first?” Then walk away.
- **When someone asks if they are right:** “Look at the room.”
- **When someone is stuck alone:** Pair them. Do not rescue them.
- **Closing:** “The photos are the lesson.”

---

## Part 5 · What to capture, and how

You are not grading. You are observing the learning process. That observation is research for building AiR.

| Evidence | What it reveals | How you capture it |
|---|---|---|
| First action | Default problem-solving behavior | One line per person in the notebook within the first two minutes |
| First AI request | How they think of AI | Ask them to screenshot it, or write it down word for word |
| Abandoned attempts | Experimentation tolerance | The discard box. Ask people to drop, not delete |
| Tool switching | Adaptability | Tally marks: who changed tools after the disrupt |
| Questions asked of other people | Collaboration | Note who asks whom. Not what they ask |
| What they save | What they value | Ask everyone to name one thing they kept |
| Final decision | Judgment | The finished thing, photographed |
| Second attempt | Whether learning happened | The Evolve photo against the Expose photo |

Rules for capture:

- Photograph the room, not faces, when you can. If faces, get consent before the session.
- Never publish a photo with a name attached without permission.
- Never upload camera frames or private details to an AI tool.
- Students do not need to be told these are being watched. They do need to be told nothing is graded.

After the session, put the two room photos side by side. Count who moved. Write one paragraph: what surprised you. That paragraph is next week’s design brief.

## Part 6 · Designing a new situation

Use this card. If you cannot fill every line, the situation is not ready.

```
NAME
CONDITIONS   What is in the room. What is missing on purpose.
SAY          The one thing you say out loud. One sentence.
WATCH        Three behaviors you are looking for.
DISRUPT      What you remove, and when.
RUN IT AGAIN What you repeat to see if behavior changed.
THE TEST     Could this exist in a normal classroom?  Y / N
```

If the test is Y, change one condition until it is N. Common fixes: remove the instruction, remove the obvious tool, make the goal impossible, put the choice on the floor instead of on paper.

## Part 7 · When it goes sideways

- **Nobody moves at the stations.** Count down louder. At zero, say “Not choosing is a choice,” and photograph the room anyway.
- **Everyone picks the same station.** Good. That is your disrupt. Remove that option for the challenge.
- **Someone explains AI to the group.** Let them finish one sentence, then: “Show us instead.” Hand them materials.
- **Someone says it is cheating.** “Using a calculator on a test where it is not allowed is cheating. Using one to build a bridge is engineering. This is the bridge room.” Move on.
- **A team declares the mystery unsolvable.** Ask which part of the question they think is wrong. That is a valid finding. Write it down.
- **A kid and an adult are in the same room.** Same challenge, same stations. Age does not decide depth. Pair them if they would not choose each other.
- **The AI tool goes down.** You just got a free disrupt. Say nothing.
- **Running long.** Cut Collide or the Flip. Never cut Expose or Evolve.

---

## Part 8 · Your own training loop

You learn this the same way they do.

1. **Expose yourself.** Before your first session, take the ten-second choice and the five walls yourself. Photograph it.
2. **Run one situation** with three people, not fifteen. Capture everything.
3. **Disrupt yourself.** Run it again without your notes.
4. **Collide.** Co-run with someone from a different world: a coach, a librarian, a barber.
5. **Reflect.** Leave your own evidence: voice note, photo of the room, the situation card you threw out.
6. **Evolve.** Repeat your own ten-second choice after five sessions. See if you moved.

You are ready to run a full room when you can go ninety minutes without explaining anything.

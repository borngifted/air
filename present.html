Why AIR exists

Change how people approach AI —
not which buttons they press.

Clear the air. Un-clutter the mindset. Communicate with intention. AIR is a free community course from Digi2U.org for everyone from 8 to 100. It is not a tool tour and not a certificate mill. You start with a real human need, treat AI as a creative partner, apply design judgment to everything it gives you back, and finish work that someone actually uses. Every lesson below is taught through creative AI workflows that were really run — image-anchored films chained frame to frame, custom-trained models, logo-led design systems — with the seams left visible so you learn to see them.

The course · eight lessons

One journey. Eight moves.
Real workflows, seams visible.

No certificates, no tests, no grades — each lesson ends with something you made. Pick your level inside every lesson: Explore (guided, first-timers, families) · Create (your own subject, real decisions) · Build (under the hood: workflow, code, production systems).

01

Clear the Air

Move from fear, hype and overload to one meaningful goal.

Workflow: the mission sentence — "I want to make ___ so that ___ can ___." Everything else in AIR needs this ticket first. Try it below.

  • Explore — say your sentence out loud to somebody.
  • Create — write it, then cut it in half.
  • Build — write three, rank by who is actually waiting for it.
02

See the Possibility

Understand what makes finished work useful — not impressive, useful.

Workflow: the chained film — eight brand stills morphed into one motion piece by matching each clip's first frame to the last frame before it. The film behind this page was made that way.

  • Explore — watch one finished AI piece and name who it helps.
  • Create — collect three finished works and write what made each land.
  • Build — take one apart: list every human decision inside it.
03

Choose Your Mission

Name the person, the problem, the message, the outcome.

Workflow: the human-before-tool brief — who is this for, what should they know, feel or do, which decisions need human judgment, and is AI even appropriate here.

  • Explore — describe one real person your idea helps.
  • Create — write the four-line brief for a real project.
  • Build — add the failure plan: what happens when it breaks?
04

Direct the Machine

Speak like a creative director — not a magic-prompt hunter.

Workflow: art direction blocks — subject, light, lens, palette, mood, and a strict negative list (no robots, no neon). Decline the preset when the tool tries to swap in someone else's look.

  • Explore — direct one image with five deliberate words.
  • Create — write a reusable direction block for your project's look.
  • Build — chain outputs: feed one result in as the next input.
05

Make It Clear

Let hierarchy, contrast and space do the talking.

Workflow: the logo-led system — this site's every color, angle and motion comes from the mark itself. Two tokens were checked for contrast before anything shipped.

  • Explore — find the one sentence that matters most; make it biggest.
  • Create — one page, four sizes of type, nothing centered by accident.
  • Build — tokens: name your colors and spacing, use only the names.
06

Challenge the Result

Critique, verify, question, improve. Judgment is the work.

Workflow: the seams exercise — an early cut of this course's film drifted: the person changed between clips, the poster text was gibberish. It was kept, on purpose, to learn from.

  • Explore — find three things wrong with an AI image you like.
  • Create — accuracy, representation, readability: check all three, fix one.
  • Build — break your own work deliberately and watch what it tells the user.
07

Build Your Way

Turn what worked into your own repeatable workflow.

Workflow: the process that built this film became a written, reusable skill the same day — generate, extract frames, chain, publish. Run twice means it's real.

  • Explore — list your steps from the last thing you finished.
  • Create — write them so somebody else could run them.
  • Build — make the repeat one command.
08

Put It in the World

Complete something real and let someone use it.

Workflow: ship on your own ground — this presentation lives on a public page anyone can visit, owned outright, no platform between the work and the world.

  • Explore — show a finished thing to one person; write what they said.
  • Create — publish one thing with a real URL.
  • Build — make it self-contained so no vanished service can break it.
